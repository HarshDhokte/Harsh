The problem

The client's welfare benefits ran on paper. To claim, an employee checked the rules in a long policy document, filled in a form and worked out the amount by hand.

My role

I was a UX/UI design intern on the project for a month. I designed the scheme application flow and its edge cases, explored retiree onboarding, and read the scheme rulebooks to work out what each screen needed.

The result

The same five-step application works for every scheme. The portal was due to go live at the end of my internship.

00 · Context

A welfare benefits system for one of India's largest conglomerates.

The client is a large Indian group with many companies under it and more than 30,000 employees. It runs its own welfare benefits for employees, retirees and their families, from medical and hospitalisation cover to scholarships.

Until this project, all of it ran by hand. An employee who wanted to claim for a hospital stay had to read the rules, fill in a paper form and attach the bills. There was no old system to redesign, so the portal was designed from scratch.

01 · Problem

Every rule lived in one 80-page policy document.

The client is paying out money, so each scheme checks a lot before it pays: who is eligible, how much they can get and what proof they need. My team received all of it as a rulebook of more than 80 pages.

What the document held

Each scheme had its own eligibility criteria, limits, documents and approval steps. An employee had to find the right scheme and read its rules before applying.

Where the rules clashed

Some rules didn't fit the scheme they belonged to. One education scheme pays for courses recognised by the UGC or the government, yet it also lists professional courses such as CA, which the UGC doesn't approve. The document never says what happens to a CA student.

My main task was to take these rules, gaps included, and explain them to an employee clearly enough that they could apply on their own.

For one claim, an employee had to answer four questions.

01

Which scheme applies to me?

Several schemes overlap in what they cover.

02

Am I eligible?

It depends on employment status, years of service, which family member is claiming, age and insurance.

03

What do I submit?

Each scheme asks for its own bills, reports and certificates.

04

What happens next?

The claim goes through several reviews before payment, and the employee can't see any of them.

How might the portal explain these rules so that an employee can apply without help?

Most schemes only pay what's left after insurance. I handled this with one Yes or No question inside the claim form.

05 · Story 03 · Retiree onboarding

Retirees needed their own way to sign in.

Active employees log in with their company HR account, which retirees lose when they leave. Five of the seven medical schemes still cover retirees, so they needed another way in.

07 · Reflection

What I learned.

Reading the rules came first.

Most screen decisions came from a specific clause in the rulebook.

Unclear rules became questions.

When a clause was vague, I took it to the team, and their answer shaped the screen.

Paper steps needed design too.

Claims still end with a courier envelope, so the printable letter was part of the flow.
