Context

Welfare benefits for a company of 30,000+ people

I spent my internship designing for one of India's largest conglomerates. Its employees, retirees and their families use this portal to claim welfare benefits, from medical cover to scholarships.

Before the portal, every claim was manual. People read the policy, filled in a paper form, attached their bills and sent it all in.

The task was to bring every scheme into one portal and keep each one's eligibility rules and exceptions intact.

There was no old system to redesign. Our job was to build the whole thing from scratch, starting from an 80-page rulebook.

My part: I worked on translating complex welfare policies into clear claim journeys, resolving gaps in existing designs, and exploring a separate onboarding experience for retired employees.

Each scheme had its own eligibility, limits, documents and approvals. I went through them one by one.

2

Asking the same five questions

Who can apply? What's covered? What details, what proof, and what happens after? Asking these of every scheme made them easy to compare.

3

Flagging rules that didn't fit

Some rules clashed with their own scheme. I noted each one and took it to the team before drawing the screen.

Research · What came up

Most of the difficulty was hidden inside the rules

The client is paying out money, so every scheme checks a lot before it pays. Employees had to work all of it out on their own.

A rule that didn't fit

Who counts as a student?

One scholarship pays only for UGC or government-recognised courses. The same scheme also lists professional courses like CA, which the UGC doesn't approve.

The document never says what happens to a CA student.

Problem

To make one claim, an employee had to decode an 80-page rulebook alone. They couldn't tell which scheme applied, whether they qualified or what to send, and an incomplete claim could be rejected.

What I designed · 03

Retirees needed a different onboarding flow

Active employees

HRMS profiles automatically populate the portal with family and employment details used to determine scheme eligibility.

Retirees

Without these records, they had to provide their information from scratch.

The risk Without it, the portal couldn't accurately determine their eligibility, potentially preventing them from accessing benefits they qualified for.
