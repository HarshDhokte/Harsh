But what happens when an AI agent starts taking over some of these activities?

What about the person behind the purchase?

Existing research explains what agents can do. I wanted to understand where people draw the line between their own decisions and the agent's.

Survey insights

So where would people actually let AI take over?

Someone might happily let AI reorder groceries and still want to choose their own clothes or gifts. The line sat at consequence: big financial commitments were the least trusted, everyday essentials the most.

Interview insights

How people actually shop today

Shopping isn't one behaviour. The same person can want help with one purchase and full control over the next.

Different levels of assistance

Some explore and decide alone. Others look for guidance.

"I want to know my options, but I still want to make the final decision."

Discovery or a task

For some it's an enjoyable browse. For others, a job to finish.

"I can spend hours looking at new things even when I don't urgently need anything."

The purchase shapes involvement

Routine, low-risk buys invite help. Personal or consequential ones pull people back in.

Research synthesis

Final synthesis: what the research revealed

ThemeWhat emerged across researchWhat it means for my research
DiscoveryPeople don't shop only when they have a specific need. Browsing, exploring and stumbling upon products are part of the experience.Agentic shopping should support discovery, not optimise it away.
Trust & AutonomyPeople are comfortable delegating repetitive, low-consequence purchases, but want more involvement as cost, personal importance or consequence increases.The agent needs to adapt its level of autonomy to the decision.
TransparencyPeople want to know why the agent recommended something, what it changed and when it is acting on their behalf.Important agent decisions need to be visible and understandable.
PersonalisationParticipants valued recommendations that reflected their preferences, habits, needs and context rather than generic suggestions.Personal context is what makes delegation feel relevant and trustworthy.
Human ControlPeople want AI to reduce the effort of shopping, but still want the ability to intervene, change a decision or make the final call when something matters.Automation should reduce effort without removing ownership.

Shopper archetypes

Each archetype wants a different amount of help from an agent, which meant Neo couldn't treat every purchase the same way.

03 · Defining the direction

From research to design

As people become more time-constrained and e-commerce becomes more autonomous, people may want to spend less time shopping without compromising on the quality, relevance or discovery of their choices. But as more of the process is delegated to AI, the boundary between what people let AI decide and what they want to discover, evaluate and control themselves starts to blur.

How might we

How might we design an AI shopping experience that helps people delegate purchasing decisions confidently while keeping them in control of where that boundary sits, what the agent does, and what remains theirs to decide?

My approach to every design decision

01

Understand context and intentSo a task never starts from scratch.

02

Make every decision transparentSo people know what Neo did and why.

03

Keep the user in controlWithout oversight becoming a full-time job.

04

Protect the joy of discoverySo delegation doesn't narrow what people see.

These four come from the How Might We areas in the original project.

04 · Exploring the interaction

An agent works in the background. How do you design for that?

Design decision

If an agent is meant to help across every shopping platform, why should I have to open a separate app to talk to it? That pushed Neo into the phone's system layer, alongside the apps people already use.

Design challenge

But a traditional interface exists because the user does the work. When Neo searches five stores, almost none of that is visible. My job became making the invisible visible, so people could follow Neo without supervising every step.

05 · The final experience

Introducing Neo, Agentic Shopping Agent

Task flow 1

Ordering snacks for eight from a WhatsApp message

Friends are coming over at 9, and Aarav drops a snack list in The Weekend Crew. The user presses the side button, circles his message, and Neo turns it into one order across three quick-commerce stores.

Floating overlay interface:

Neo opens as a layer over the chat instead of a separate app, so the user's context stays right where it is.

Circle to ask:

The user draws around one message. Neo reads only that selection, which keeps the scope of what it sees in the user's hands.

User decides

Editable plan:

Neo finds six items for eight people, needed before 9, and shows them as a list the user can edit before any search begins.

Thoughts panel for transparency:

Store logos show exactly where Neo is looking, and a two-line log keeps progress readable instead of becoming a wall of text.

Scalable summary cards:

The order is grouped by store with each delivery time, so one card explains three separate orders at a glance.

One deliberate approval:

The Universal Cart names each store as the seller. Slide to pay and Face ID sign the exact cart, so nothing is bought without the user.

User approves

Live Activity:

Once paid, each rider is tracked from the Dynamic Island, and Neo offers to update the group.

Asks before remembering:

Neo suggests remembering that The Weekend Crew is usually eight people, and saves it only if the user agrees.

User decides

Task flow 2

Asking out loud for a first trek kit under ₹5,000

With nothing on screen to read, the user simply says, "I'm going on my first trek this Sunday. Get me everything I need and keep it under ₹5,000." Neo plans the kit, then takes the long search into the Dynamic Island.

Source pills build trust:

Neo flags light rain at Rajmachi and shows the forecast as the source, then lets the user keep or skip the rain gear.

User decides

Hand-off to the background:

Neo explains what it will check, then offers one action: keep using the phone.

Real-time agent status:

The Dynamic Island moves through searching each store, comparing 23 options and checking delivery dates, each in its own colour.

Non-intrusive by default:

The island expands only when the cart is ready, with Later and Review cart, so Neo never takes over the screen uninvited.

One decision, surfaced:

The shoes arrive at 9 PM the night before a 6 AM start. Neo offers Friday delivery for ₹99 more, and the user picks.

User decides

Tracking after purchase:

Five orders, one Live Activity, each with its delivery day.

Task flow 3

Booking an airport cab by voice, with a stop for Ajay

A meeting runs late before a 7:10 PM flight. The user asks for a cab to the airport at 4:30 with a stop for Ajay. Neo connects the flight time and Ajay's address, books the ride, and offers to tell Ajay.

Visualising the process:

The Thoughts panel shows each step, from reading the flight time to comparing Uber, Ola and Rapido. Ride and pickup come pre-selected from memory.

Confirmation & control:

The plan arrives as a familiar booking card. The Book button carries the price, and Change the ride stays one tap away.

User approves

Actionable prompts:

The offer to message Ajay is a simple Yes or No, which keeps social actions in the user's hands.

User decides

Action in view:

WhatsApp opens and the message sends in front of the user, marked "Sent by Neo for you".

Task flow 4

Neo remembers the speaker Rohan asked for

Rohan asked the user to bring a Bluetooth speaker tonight. Neo connects that chat with tonight's plan and offers to handle it, and it explains where the idea came from before doing anything.

Non-intrusive notification:

The Super Suggestion arrives as a small card with Not now and Handle it, and it doesn't demand immediate action.

User decides

Context snippet for trust:

Neo shows the calendar event and Rohan's exact message, so the user can see why Neo is suggesting this.

Three clear options:

Recommended, cheapest and fastest speakers, all arriving before 9 and under ₹2,000.

Memory you can see and edit:

Every item shows how Neo learned it: I observed, I inferred or You told me. Inferred items wait for Save, Edit or Don't save.

User decides
